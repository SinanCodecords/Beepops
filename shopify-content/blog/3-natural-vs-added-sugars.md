## Content (paste below this line)

Sugar isn't the enemy. But not all sugar is the same. Understanding the difference between natural and added sugars is one of the simplest ways to eat better without giving up the sweetness you love.

### What are natural sugars?

Natural sugars are found inside whole foods, like fructose in fruit and lactose in milk. They come packaged with fibre, water, vitamins, and minerals, which slow down how quickly the sugar enters your blood. That's why an apple keeps you going far longer than a spoonful of sugar does.

### What are added sugars?

Added sugars are put into food during processing or cooking: white sugar, glucose syrup, corn syrup, and many other names you'll find on labels. They add calories but very little nutrition. Many "healthy" biscuits, breakfast cereals, flavoured yoghurts, and packaged juices are loaded with them.

### How much is too much?

The World Health Organization recommends keeping free sugars below 10% of your daily energy intake, and ideally below 5%. That's roughly 25 grams, or about 6 teaspoons, a day for an adult. A single soft drink can go well past that.

### How to spot hidden sugar on a label

- Check the ingredient list. Ingredients are listed from most to least, so sugar near the top means a lot of it.
- Watch for other names: sucrose, dextrose, maltose, glucose syrup, invert syrup.
- Compare "sugars" per 100g across products, not per serving.

### Where honey and jaggery fit in

Honey and jaggery still count as sugar, so they're best enjoyed in moderation. But compared with refined white sugar, they're less processed and come with small amounts of minerals and, in raw honey, natural enzymes and antioxidants. That's why we sweeten BeePops bars with honey and jaggery instead of refined sugar or syrups.

### The sweet truth

You don't need to cut out sweetness completely. Choose whole foods first, read labels, and pick snacks sweetened the natural way. Small swaps add up to a big difference.
